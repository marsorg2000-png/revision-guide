Elizabeth's Government, Court & Early Life

👑 Early Life & Challenges

  • Birth: Born 1533 to Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn; being a girl was seen as a disappointment. Mother executed for treason/witchcraft when Elizabeth was 2.
  • Education: Educated for court life rather than rule (learned French, Italian, Latin, Greek, music). Seen as a threat by Mary I during her reign. Sent to stay with Catherine Parr, strengthening Protestant views.
  • Accession (1558): Crowned Queen at 25 inheriting massive debts from Mary's wars, religious division, trade collapse, and a hostile France/Spain.

🏛️ Structure of Government

  • Monarch: Held supreme authority, declared war/peace, called/dissolved Parliament.
  • Privy Council: ~19 key advisors run daily government. Controlled by Secretary of State (William Cecil, then Francis Walsingham). Used "divide and rule", small size, and written records to maintain control.
  • Parliament: House of Lords (nobles/bishops) & House of Commons (gentry). Approved taxes and laws. Elizabeth controlled it via Royal Veto, managing agendas, and imprisoning outspoken MPs like Peter Wentworth.
  • Local Government: Justices of the Peace (JPs) maintained law/order locally; Lord Lieutenants raised local militias and collected taxes.

The Religious Settlement & Catholic Threats

⛪ Elizabethan Religious Settlement (1559)

  • Act of Supremacy (1559): Made Elizabeth "Supreme Governor" of the Church of England (avoiding "Head" title to appease Catholics).
  • Act of Uniformity (1559): Made church attendance compulsory using the Book of Common Prayer; fines for recusants (Catholics refusing to attend).
  • Royal Injunctions (1559): 57 rules removing "superstitious" relics while keeping some traditional vestments.
  • Thirty-Nine Articles (1563): Defined moderate Protestant doctrine.

⚔️ Catholic Plots & Rebellions

  • Northern Rebellion (1569): Earls of Northumberland and Westmorland tried to depose Elizabeth and put Mary Queen of Scots on the throne; crushed, 450 rebels executed.
  • Papal Bull (Regnans in Excelsis, 1570): Pope excommunicated Elizabeth, absolving Catholics from allegiance and triggering harsher anti-Catholic laws.
  • Ridolfi Plot (1571): Italian banker plotted to marry Mary to the Duke of Norfolk and overthrow Elizabeth; discovered, Norfolk executed.
  • Throckmorton (1583) & Babington (1586) Plots: Assassination plots uncovered by Walsingham's spy network, leading directly to Mary's trial and execution.

Mary Queen of Scots & The Spanish Armada (1588)

👑 Mary, Queen of Scots

  • Fled to England in 1568 after being overthrown by Scottish Protestant lords following husband Darnley's murder and marriage to Bothwell.
  • Acted as the natural Catholic focal point for treasonous plots against Elizabeth.
  • Tried after the Babington Plot and executed at Fotheringhay Castle in February 1587.

🚢 The Spanish Armada (1588)

  • Causes: Philip II's Catholic zeal, English piracy (Drake), support for Dutch Protestant rebels, and Mary's execution.
  • The Plan: 130 ships sailed to collect 30,000 troops from the Netherlands under Duke of Parma.
  • Why it Failed: English superior fire-ships broke formation at Calais; agile English galleons outmaneuvered Spanish ships; storms ("Protestant Wind") wrecked retreating ships around Scotland/Ireland.
  • Significance: Boosted English national confidence and cemented naval supremacy.

Society, Culture & The Golden Age

🎭 Culture & The Golden Age

  • Theatre: Boom in purpose-built theatres (The Red Lion 1567, The Globe 1599). William Shakespeare wrote ~40 plays. Banned inside London city walls due to plague and Puritan opposition, so built in Southwark.
  • Architecture & Gentry: Rise of the gentry who built magnificent stately homes (Hardwick Hall) displaying wealth. Nobility sidelined in favor of loyal gentry officials.
  • Propaganda & Cult of Gloriana: Portraits (globes, pearls) and poetry (Spenser's The Faerie Queene) projected Elizabeth as a divine, protective virgin queen.

👥 Hierarchy & Daily Life

  • Great Chain of Being: Strict hierarchical order from God down to peasants.
  • Marriage & Succession: Constant pressure on Elizabeth to marry to produce an heir and prevent civil war. She used marriage negotiations for diplomatic leverage while protecting her independence.
  • Essex Rebellion (1601): Robert Devereux, Earl of Essex, rebelled after losing monopoly and favor; quick 12-hour failure resulting in his execution.

Poverty, Elizabethan Poor Laws & Exploration

🍞 The Problem of Poverty

  • Causes: Population rise (2.8m to 4m), inflation from Henry VIII's debased coinage, enclosure of land for sheep farming, collapse of wool trade, and bad harvests (1594–98).
  • Distinctions: Divided into "impotent poor" (deserving elderly/sick) and "able-bodied idle poor" (vagrants/beggars feared for social disorder).
  • Elizabethan Poor Law (1601): First national poor relief system. Taxed locals to build almshouses for the impotent poor, provided work materials for the able-bodied, and whipped/imprisoned persistent vagrants in Houses of Correction.

🧭 Exploration & Trade

  • Explorers: Sir Francis Drake completed first circumnavigation (1577–80), raiding Spanish treasure ships; Walter Raleigh established Roanoke colony (1585); John Hawkins engaged in slave trade and naval ship design.
  • Motives: Escape Spanish trade monopolies, acquire New World silver/spices, advance navigation, and spread Protestantism.

Mastering AQA Elizabethan England Exam Questions

Exam Frameworks

📝 Q1: Source Utility (8 Marks)

"How useful is Source A..."

  • Para 1 (Content): Describe/quote source + make inference + contextual knowledge.
  • Para 2 (Provenance): WHO, WHEN, WHAT, WHY evaluation.

📝 Q2: Interpretations (8 Marks)

"How convincing is Interpretation A about..."

  • Analysis: Identify the main argument of the interpretation.
  • Support: Use specific historical details from the passage that are correct.
  • Challenge/Refine: Use counter-evidence or alternative aspects to show where it is less convincing.

📝 Q3/Q4: Narrative / Essay (16 Marks)

"Write an account of..." or "How far do you agree..."

  • Structure with chronological sequencing or balanced PEEL paragraphs addressing multiple factors and a clear conclusion.

Full Historical Glossary & Key Terms

Complete definitions of every essential Elizabethan history term.

Armada

A large fleet of warships sent by Spain in 1588 to invade England.

Excommunication

Expulsion from the Catholic Church by the Pope, ordered against Elizabeth in 1570.

Gentry

Landowners who grew in wealth and political influence during the Elizabethan era without holding noble titles.

Puritan

An extreme Protestant who wanted to purify the Church of England of all Catholic elements.

Recusants

Catholics who refused to attend compulsory Protestant church services.

Vagabond

A homeless, unemployed person who travelled from place to place looking for work or begging.
